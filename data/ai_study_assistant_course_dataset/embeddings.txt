Embeddings

An embedding is a numerical representation of information. Text is converted into a vector containing numbers that capture useful semantic relationships learned by an embedding model.

For example, sentences such as:
"How do embeddings work?"
and
"Explain text embeddings"
may have vectors that are closer to each other than vectors for an unrelated sentence about cooking.

Similarity between embeddings can be used to find semantically related text. A common similarity measure is cosine similarity, which compares the direction of two vectors.

A simplified workflow is:

Text
-> embedding model
-> vector
-> similarity comparison

Embeddings are commonly used in semantic search, document retrieval, recommendation systems, clustering, and duplicate detection.

An embedding is not the original text. It is a numerical representation that can be stored and compared. The quality of retrieval depends on the embedding model, the text being embedded, and the similarity method used.
